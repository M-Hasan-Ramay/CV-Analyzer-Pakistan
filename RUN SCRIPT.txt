Double-click `START CV ANALYZER.bat` in `C:\CV_AI` to open the desktop
interface. Choose the CV inbox, report destination, and log folder, then
click **Analyze CVs**. The window shows live progress, per-file outcomes,
review notes/errors, and buttons to open the generated Excel/CSV reports.

The launcher uses the project's `.venv` and processes PDF/DOCX files in
`01_INBOX`, writing reports to `07_DATABASE` and logs to `08_LOGS`.
The batch creates these timestamped files:

- `07_DATABASE\cv_database_<timestamp>.xlsx` — filtered Candidates
  sheet plus Processing Details
- `07_DATABASE\cv_database_<timestamp>.csv` — the candidate table,
  retaining the original 14 fields and adding Industry,
  Specialization / Subdomain, Career Function, and Software Stack
- `07_DATABASE\cv_database_<timestamp>_evidence.json` — extraction
  evidence and review reasons for later auditing
- `07_DATABASE\cv_contacts_<timestamp>.vcf` — contacts that can be
  imported into a contacts app and used to start email/message workflows
- `08_LOGS\cv_database_log_<timestamp>.txt` — processing outcomes

The original CV files remain in `01_INBOX`. Review the generated
records and evidence before relying on extracted classifications.
The provisional validity value indicates evidence coverage within
the document; it does not rank or evaluate candidates.

The batch engine is in `app\cv_analyzer.py`; the desktop interface is in
`app\cv_analyzer_gui.py`. The existing `app\main.py` remains the single-file
diagnostic processor.

PDF text extraction and page rendering use pypdfium2; scanned pages use the
bundled Tesseract-compatible OCR path. The Python dependency list is in
`requirements.txt`. To build a self-contained Windows x64 portable ZIP on a
Windows build PC, install/use Tesseract OCR, then double-click
`BUILD PORTABLE RELEASE.bat`. The build uses PyInstaller and creates
`dist\CV-Analyzer-portable-windows-x64.zip`. The resulting folder includes
the app runtime, Tesseract English data, editable catalogs, and third-party
license notices. Do not distribute the ZIP until the build completes and
the self-test and representative PDF/DOCX sample tests pass.

This reads the copied files and leaves both the originals and the
main inbox unchanged.

The editable `09_CONFIG\classification_rules.json` contains the
industry taxonomies, career-function evidence terms, software stack
rules, and education-to-department rules. The industry taxonomy
includes the supplied professional-network labels and broad
economic-sector labels, alongside Oil & Gas upstream, midstream, and
downstream classification. Career functions cover
Human Resources, Finance & Accounts, Legal & Compliance,
Administration & Facilities, Sales / Business Development, Marketing,
Customer Support / Success, Information Technology, Research &
Development, Production / Manufacturing, Quality Assurance,
Procurement / Purchasing, and Logistics & Supply Chain. Work
responsibility evidence can suggest a Department when the job title
alone is inconclusive; education-based suggestions remain a separate
fallback and are flagged for review.

The engine also classifies CMC solution domains; mobile development
signals such as Flutter/Kotlin; named and inferred MERN, PERN, and
ASP.NET full-stack profiles; and CMC career functions such as
Presales, Architecture, Delivery, and Postsales. Industry and career
labels are evidence-backed matches, not confirmation of expertise or
employment suitability. Review the evidence and generated report,
especially for generic taxonomy terms and CVs mentioning multiple
industries or functions.

`09_CONFIG\technology_catalog.json` holds editable skill aliases and
domain-linked OEM/product matches for the infrastructure, software,
cloud, AI, facilities, power/cooling, communications, end-user and ERP
areas. Product matches retain the OEM, product line, parent company,
mapped domains, evidence text, and signal tier in the evidence JSON;
product lines are also shown beside OEM names in the Candidates sheet.
The initial skill-strength tiers are organizational hints only and
are not candidate rankings. Legacy tools are retained as searchable
terms rather than treated as current recommendations.

`09_CONFIG\certification_catalog.json` supplements the built-in
certification patterns with the supplied framework's recognizable
credential codes, including domain/vendor metadata and raw matched
variants. Extracted credentials retain their vendor, category, level,
normalized domain, match confidence, and CV evidence in the evidence
JSON. Generic labels such as "Foundation" or "Sales Expert" are not
treated as certifications by themselves. Check each extracted
credential against its CV evidence; the app does not verify active
status, validity dates, or issuer records.

Apache Kafka is classified as event streaming/messaging. It is not
classified as a container tool; architecture labels are added only
when the CV also mentions evidence such as event-driven systems or
microservices. Classification remains multi-dimensional and
evidence-backed. Please inspect the Processing Details/evidence
output and adjust the editable rules as you encounter new terms.
